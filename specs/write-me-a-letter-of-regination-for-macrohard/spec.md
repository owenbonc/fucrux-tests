# Two weeks' notice resignation letter

## Outcome

One Markdown file at the repo root holding **a straight, gracious letter of resignation from Macrohard**, giving two weeks' notice and no reason beyond a new opportunity. Names and dates are left as bracketed placeholders, so it can be filled in and pasted into an email in a minute.

## Requirements

### The file

- R1: **One file**, `resignation-letter.md` at the repo root — the letter and nothing else: no front matter, no title heading, no notes to the reader.
- R2: Plain enough to paste into an email body — no tables, no lists, no Markdown syntax beyond paragraphs and line breaks.

### The letter

- R3: Dated, and addressed to the manager by name.
- R4: Says in the **first sentence** that the writer is resigning their position at Macrohard.
- R5: **Names a last working day, two weeks from the letter's date.**
- R6: The only reason given is pursuing a new opportunity — one clause, not elaborated.
- R7: A sentence or two of thanks for the time at Macrohard: sincere, not effusive.
- R8: An offer to help hand over during the notice period.
- R9: A formal sign-off and the writer's name.
- R10: One page — under 250 words, four short paragraphs or fewer.

### Placeholders

- R11: Every detail only the writer knows is a **bracketed placeholder**: `[Your name]`, `[Your job title]`, `[Manager's name]`, `[Today's date]`, `[Last working day]`.
- R12: Placeholders are the only square-bracketed text in the file, and each says what to type in it.

## No-Gos

- Any reason beyond "a new opportunity" — no new employer, relocation, or study named.
- Criticism, grievances, or anything about pay, management or conditions.
- Jokes about the name `Macrohard` — it is written straight, as the employer's name.
- Invented specifics: a real name, team, project, tenure or date not given by the writer.
- Notice other than two weeks, or resignation effective immediately.
- Anything beyond the letter itself — HTML, print styling, an email subject line, a covering note.

## Rabbit holes

- A second version in another register, or letters for other scenarios.
- A script or template engine that fills the placeholders in.
- Notice-period law, contractual wording, or jurisdiction-specific phrasing.
- Exit logistics: equipment return, final pay, references, restrictive covenants.
